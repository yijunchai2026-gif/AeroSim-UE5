# Coastal visual review

Review date: 2026-10-17. Scope: the user's rejected coastal prototype and its naturalism revision. This is a visual direction and defect record, not AAA certification or user acceptance. Ownership: UI/art review only; implementation is covered by `adr/PACIFIC_COAST_NATURALISM.md`.

## Observed baseline

The packaged overview in the local NaturalEvidence before capture shows recognisable sky, clouds, land, trees and sea, but their shape language is inconsistent with a natural coast. The visible trees repeat three perfect cones. Mountain slopes look like smooth, glossy cones or melted mounds. The sea reads as a uniform cyan sheet. Large empty grass areas are uninterrupted by believable local relief. Small shore rocks form an evenly spaced necklace instead of a broken talus apron.

G1 fails in this image: a straight exposed edge at the left rear ends at plateau height; the ocean also has a visible straight boundary. The land therefore reads as a suspended slab. An attractive overview cannot establish that unseen sides are supported. Fix and inspect the complete perimeter before surface polish.

## Required shape corrections

| Element | Concrete direction | Reject when |
|---|---|---|
| Land and cliff | A continuous headland with supported roots below the waterline on every exposed edge. Broad headland bends, secondary notches and local erosion variation; plateau meets cliff without cracks. Mountain foothills grow out of the same terrain. | Any open edge, missing underside, water boundary or floating contour is visible from the side, rear or flight route. |
| Forest | Coastal conifers with visible trunks, asymmetric lateral branches and broken foliage clusters. Vary crown width, trunk lean, height and branch density deterministically. Arrange overlapping groups with small glades and sparse forest edges. Keep the route and boardwalk readable. | Trees read as stacked cones, identical Christmas trees, a uniform planted grid, floating bases or intersecting the route. |
| Mountains | One legible main ridge with lower secondary ridges and branching gullies. Distinguish the large landform from its smaller surface variation. Blend the foothills into the headland. Use broad, uneven saddles rather than a row of repeated isolated peaks. | Repeated smooth humps, equal peaks, melted slopes, glossy highlights or disconnected mountain islands dominate the view. |
| Sea | Open water extending beyond all visible land. A calm deep-water base with broad low swells and smaller nonuniform ripples; subtle depth/shore colour variation and broken narrow surf at rock contacts. The horizon remains level and uninterrupted. | It reads as a flat cyan floor, giant regular grid, repeated white stripes, visible mesh boundary, or waves intersect the cliff as disconnected floating objects. |
| Shore rock | Irregular groups rooted at the cliff foot, with varied sizes and gaps, partly submerged; rockfall direction follows the cliff. | Equally spaced cubes, a decorative border, floating boulders or unrelated rocks across inland terrain. |

Dark tree silhouettes must retain some internal separation at normal flight height. Mountain colour should distinguish near foothills from distant ridges without whitening every crest. These surface judgements follow G1 and G2 shape checks, not substitute for them.

## User-directed Umunhum reference revision

The user clarified that keyboard control works; mouse control is untested and is not the priority. Current priority is the scene: the incongruous boardwalk, artificial sea and mountains inspired by Bay Area Mount Umunhum. The UI reviewer inspected the actual packaged `v3-overview-frame-023.png`, `v3-cliff-frame-023.png` and `v3-forest-frame-023.png`. Photographed masked needles now give the near forest an open canopy, unlike the rejected opaque volume version. The boardwalk still reads as a freestanding fence across the field, the hillside as two smooth rounded mounds, and the water as a uniformly rippled blue sheet. Material improvement alone has not resolved those composition and landform defects.

Reference facts: Sierra Azul's official description identifies ridges, ravines and a mosaic of grasslands, chaparral, bay/oak woodland and locally pine; those vegetation communities, rather than an all-pine mountain, inform the design. [Sierra Azul Preserve](https://www.openspace.org/preserves/sierra-azul). The summit was historically graded and later restored with recontouring and native planting; a small broad summit shoulder is therefore a possible reference detail, not a reason to flatten the entire ridge. [Mount Umunhum Summit](https://www.openspace.org/what-we-do/projects/mount-umunhum-summit). The following implementation is artistic inference for a fictional coast inspired by those landforms, not a geographic reconstruction or a claim that Umunhum sits at this cliff.

Apply the revision in this order:

1. **G0, reason for the path:** replace the long isolated elevated walkway with a short footbridge across a visible shallow drainage swale. Keep the rest a ground trail. A proposed 24–40 m bridge and 1.8–2.2 m deck width are artistic scale choices, not engineering standards. Both ends must lead somewhere: connect to a continuous dirt route that approaches the training clearing and continues into the forest. If there is no obstacle beneath a section, keep that section on the ground. A bridge across a randomly flat lawn remains a failure.
2. **G1, construction and terrain:** set the bridge deck slightly above the swale banks, add load-bearing beams and posts that visibly reach the sampled land, and blend each approach into a short ramp with no floating end or buried lip. Keep rail height around 1.05 m as a scene-scale target and put rails along the two deck edges. Preserve the already repaired full coastal perimeter and submerged land support. Do not fix the boardwalk by covering its unsupported pieces with shrubs.
3. **G2, Umunhum-inspired silhouette:** establish one dominant broad summit with an uneven shoulder, offset secondary masses and at least two branching valleys extending downhill. Avoid equal sinusoidal humps, a row of cones, tiny high-frequency peaks, or an uninterrupted level ridge. The summit should read clearly from the normal flight route as well as the overview. Give secondary ridges broad upper slopes and narrower gullies; relief must come from geometry before normal-map detail.
4. **G2/G3, vegetation and surface zones:** use dry grass/low chaparral on exposed shoulders, darker woodland in sheltered valleys, and limited conifer groves at appropriate slopes. Keep boundaries irregular and broad, with transition widths spanning many terrain cells. Place exposed rock locally on steep sections rather than wrapping all high ground in a hard colour band. Maintain route clearance. The material/vegetation mosaic should communicate slope and exposure; it must not become a smooth green or uniformly photographed grass carpet.
5. **G3, coastal water:** first establish a calm blue-grey open sea with a horizon and believable large scale swells, then add weaker crossing ripples. Near the cliff, concentrate broken moving foam and turbulence around actual rock contacts and recessed coves; leave gaps. A string of evenly spaced white dots or a continuous bright ribbon is a failure. Wet rock should darken close to water and transition upward. Use modest reflection and distance filtering so fine normals do not become a field of silver glitter. These are visual goals; a shader or texture change alone does not prove a working ocean system.

Required real captures: unchanged overview for comparison; a bridge approach at normal flight height showing the path/deck/ground connections; a side angle showing posts reaching ground; a view along the revised mountain ridge; a low offshore view showing wave and cliff interaction; both existing flank closure views. A still cannot establish wave motion or temporal stability, so retain the hold/move/hold sample. Freeze the same build and environment metadata. Until those views exist, the proposed revision is NOT_RUN; no visual PASS, user acceptance or AAA result is pre-signed.

## V6 direction — coastal inspection launch worksite

G0 proposal: a small, temporarily unoccupied inspection staging area connected to the existing walking route. A 6–8 m clear launch area can sit in an irregular approximately 14 m by 12 m compacted-soil/gravel work surface; this is a scene-layout proposal, not a real operational safety standard. Put a 1.5–2 m dark low landing mat at its centre rather than treating the entire area as a miniature runway or helicopter pad. Keep the sea-facing departure corridor open. Place the operator's working position 4–6 m behind and to one side of the mat. One or two small supported equipment cases (approximately 0.6 m by 0.4 m by 0.3 m) and an optional 1.0 m by 0.5 m by 0.75 m utility table explain the location. Do not add featureless human blocks or pretend unfinished primitives are final personnel/equipment assets.

G1 construction: mat thickness should be about 2 cm or less, with continuous support from the sampled land beneath. Gravel blends into earth and grass across an irregular perimeter instead of ending as a raised hard slab. All case, table, post and wind-indicator feet must touch actual terrain. A modest wind sock can justify wind orientation if its pole, base and fabric form are recognizable; avoid a floating arrow or a red rectangular stand-in. Connect a 1.2–1.8 m dirt approach to the existing forest/bridge trail, including a visible join rather than two separate path fragments. Avoid the mat, control position and departure corridor with all equipment and tree placement.

G2 normal-view review requires: (a) a low camera at the mat edge to expose thick raised slabs or gaps, (b) a roughly 1.6 m operator-height oblique view showing mat, cases and accessible approach, (c) a 5 m takeoff-height view looking back to show equipment scale and the clear departure direction, and (d) the unchanged coastal overview to show that debug gates/numbers/scale blocks no longer dominate the natural setting. Review near and far views from the same packaged build; also verify the actual starting camera and vehicle position, because a decorative mat that the demo does not start from does not resolve the user's complaint.

V6 implementation and these actual views are NOT_RUN at this design stage. G0–G2 improvements do not establish AAA completion. The previously documented water repetition, habitat sparsity and shore interaction deficits remain open; adding believable worksite props cannot substitute for them.
